First release (2026-10-11). Derived from the reference model of `extract_references`: devdoc
`KnowledgeStore/doc-repo/devdocs/202610/2026101002-devdoc-extract-references-processor.md` §3, the

## 1. Independence

Author the benchmark independently of `extract_references` predictions. Do not call the processor
or a preview API, copy `kb.ref_mentions`, or read its rows until the benchmark is frozen.
Production rows are predictions, not ground truth.

Recall and precision are both required. A benchmark that adds references the text does not make
understates the extractor's precision, exactly as one that misses references overstates it.

## 2. Make a full-document candidate inventory

Read every source line, including the cover, foreword, appendices, tables, notes, footnotes,
equations and figure captions. For long documents use bounded windows, tracking which line IDs have
been read. Search is a second-pass discovery aid (§4), never a replacement for reading.

Build two ledgers in the payload:

- `coverage`: non-overlapping source ranges covering every line, with section-specific review
  notes, including regions with no references.
- `candidates`: every plausible reference occurrence, with a stable local ID, exact spans,
  disposition (`accepted`, `excluded`, `duplicate`, or temporarily `uncertain`), **`rule_id`**,
  rationale, `cited_raw` as written, and the final `ref_ids`. A single line can hold many
  candidates.

`rule_id` must be one of the bold IDs in this file: an accepted candidate cites an `A` rule, an
excluded one an `X` rule, a duplicate **D1**.

### 2.1 What a reference is

A **reference** is one place where the document points at something it does not itself contain
at that place: another document, standard, law, publication, web page, dataset, software,
product, organization or person cited as a source, or another part of this same document. The
document's own words, values and statements are not references, even when they repeat what a
cited document says.

Test the occurrence, not its keywords. Ask: "Could a reader follow this pointer to one specific
resource or place?" If the answer is no (the target is an open class, or nothing at all), it is not
a reference.

**One reference = one occurrence of one target taking one kind of content.**

- Every occurrence counts, including repeated citations of the same target in different places.
- One sentence citing two targets gives two references ("GB/T 755 和 GB/T 1032", "[3, 5]").
- One sentence taking two different things from one target (a method and a limit) gives two
  references, one per `target_content`.
- One sentence citing one target twice for the same content gives one reference; the second is D1.
- A numbered range ("[12–15]") gives one reference per cited list entry, each with the same
  `cited_raw` and its own `list_entry_ref_id` **(benchmark rule)**.

### 2.2 Accept rules

| Rule | Accept |
|---|---|
| **A1** | An **in-text reference to an external, identified target**: a standard number, legal citation, DOI, URL, ISBN, title in 《》 or quotation marks, numbered or author-year citation, footnote citation, or a named resource ("the Personal Information Protection Law"). Includes foreword statements: drafting rules ("本文件按照 GB/T 1.1—2020 的规定起草": `based_on`, content `requirements`), supersession ("本文件代替 GB/T 755—2008": `supersedes`, content `general`), adoption ("修改采用 IEC 60034-1:2017": `based_on`, content `general`). |
| **A2** | An **entry of a reference list**: one per list entry (usually one per line). A normative references list (`list_kind = normative`) is a section that lists documents indispensable for applying this document (Normative references, Referenced documents, Applicable documents, 规范性引用文件). A bibliography or reference list is `informative`. Recognize the section by its purpose, not its heading. |
| **A3** | An **internal reference**: a pointer to another part of this document ("见 5.2", "按表 1 的规定", "see Annex B", "式(3)"). `mechanism = structural_reference`, `internal = true`. |
| **A4** | An **indirect reference** to **one specific** resource that the text does not name but context identifies: "该标准" after a standard was named, "according to the previous report", "按产品说明书安装" (the manufacturer's instructions for the product at hand), "合同约定的" (the contract between the named parties). `mechanism = indirect_reference`; `cited_raw` is the wording as written. |

### 2.3 Exclude rules

| Rule | Exclude | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **X1** | A **table of contents** line: a heading with a page number, listing this document's own parts. | "5 技术要求 ……… 3" |
| **X2** | The document's **own identity**: its number, title or edition on the cover, in running headers or footers, or "本文件"/"this document" referring to itself. Record its identifier once in the payload's `document_identifier` (§3.6). | "GB/T 46121—2025" on the cover; page header repeating it |
| **X3** | **Drafting and ownership metadata** of this document: proposer (提出), technical committee (归口), drafting organizations and drafters, issuing body, publication and implementation dates, ICS/CCS codes. They describe who made the document, not what it cites. | "本文件由全国 XX 标准化技术委员会归口"; "主要起草人：…" |
| **X4** | An **open class** of unidentified documents or rules: no reader could follow it to one resource. | "符合国家有关规定"; "applicable laws and regulations"; "相关标准"; "达标排放" |
| **X5** | **Not a pointer**: numbers, units, dates, quantities, clause numbering, a heading, or the caption or label of a table, figure or equation where it is defined ("表 1 技术指标" above table 1 is the table itself, not a reference to it). | "5.2 安全要求" as a heading |
| **X6** | An **actor, place or product mentioned as part of the content**, not cited as a source of content: who does something, where, or with what. These are entities, not references. | "由环卫部门负责收运"; "在北京地区"; "使用离心泵" |
| **X7** | A **notice without a target**: patent notices, disclaimers, copyright lines. | "本文件的某些内容可能涉及专利" |
| **X8** | **Permitted alternatives** with no target named and **examples** that do not cite ("e.g. a centrifuge") **(benchmark rule)**; a named alternative is A1. | "或其他等效方法" |

**D1** marks a duplicate of a retained reference: the same occurrence found twice during inventory
(overlapping windows), or a second citation of the same target for the same content within one
sentence.

A candidate that fits no rule is not a reason to accept it. Keep it `uncertain` and resolve it
before saving; if it reveals a gap in these rules, report it in `limitations` and do not save.

## 3. Label each accepted reference

Use only the governed vocabularies below. They mirror
`ChenWeb/server/api/doc-processing/reference_model.go`; `validate` enforces them.

### 3.1 Location and text

- `section`: the heading of the section the reference is in, as written (number and title); `""`
  only for text before the first heading (cover, foreword lines without a heading count as
  "前言"/"Foreword" when the document has that heading).
- `source_line_spans`: `"N"` or `"N:M"` strings, minimal, sorted, merged.
- `evidence`: exact substrings of the cited lines `[{"line": N, "quote": "…"}]`, covering the
  sentence or list entry that holds the reference.
- `cited_raw`: the citation exactly as written, with the source's spacing and dashes
  (`GB/T755-2019`, `[12]`, `(Smith et al., 2024)`, `表 1`). It must be a substring of a cited line.
- `target_title`, `target_version`: as the text gives them (`旋转电机 定额和性能`, `2019`); `""`
  otherwise. A listed entry usually gives both.
- Tables: when the reference is inside an HTML table line, also give `source_table_rows`
  (`[{"line": N, "rows": ["r2"]}]`, IDs as `ParseTableGrid` assigns them).

### 3.2 Target type and subtype

`target_type` is one of `document`, `web_resource`, `publication`, `standard`, `legal`,
`general_publication`, `software`, `data`, `media`, `communication`, `business_record`, `other`.
`target_subtype` is a governed subtype of that type (see `reference_model.go`, e.g.
`national_standard`, `industry_standard`, `international_standard`, `regulation`,
`journal_article`, `web_page`, `product_specification`, `manual`). If no governed subtype fits, use
`others` and put the proposed label in `target_subtype_label`; otherwise `target_subtype_label` is
`""`.

Guidance:

- A standard's subtype follows its publisher: ISO/IEC/ITU → `international_standard`; GB, GB/T,
  GB/Z, ANSI, BS, DIN, JIS → `national_standard`; Chinese sector codes (CJJ, HJ, NY/T, JGJ, QB/T …),
  ASTM, IEEE, SAE, UL → `industry_standard`; T/xxx group standards → `industry_standard`
  **(benchmark rule)**; a "(所有部分)"/"all parts" citation → `standard`, scope `collection`.
- Internal references: `target_type = document`; subtype `document_section` for a clause, annex or
  note, `document_element` for a table, figure or equation.
- Manufacturer's instructions → `document` / `manual`; a contract → `business_record` / `contract`.

### 3.3 Mechanism

One of `url`, `hyperlink`, `doi`, `isbn`, `issn`, `arxiv_id`, `pmid`, `standard_number`,
`rfc_number`, `legal_citation`, `patent_number`, `repository_path`, `file_path`, `relative_path`,
`anchor`, `numbered_citation`, `author_year`, `citation_key`, `footnote`, `named_reference`,
`structural_reference`, `indirect_reference`, `identifier`, `cross_reference_field`. Choose how the
pointer is **written**: a title in 《》 without a number is `named_reference`; a number with a title
is `standard_number`. `structural_reference` if and only if `internal = true`.

### 3.4 Relationships, scope, locator

- `relationships`: one or more asserted relationships, sorted: `cites`, `references`, `quotes`,
  `defines_in`, `based_on`, `implements`, `complies_with`, `requires`, `supersedes`, `amends`,
  `supports`, `refutes`, `extends`, `uses`, `depends_on`, `derived_from`, `see_also`. Never
  `contradicts`, `same_as`, `version_of`, `part_of` (derived by the system). Guidance: a criterion
  or criteria set in a requirement → `requires`; "shall comply with X" as a whole → `complies_with`;
  a method → `uses`; terms → `defines_in`; a listed entry → `references`; "see also"/NOTE → `see_also`.
  Use `references` when nothing more specific fits.
- `target_scope`: `collection`, `document`, `section`, `requirement`, `data_element`. A locator to a
  clause, annex or table → `section`; one value or row → `data_element`.
- `target_locator`: the place inside the target as written (`表 1 的 II 类`, `Clause 7.5`,
  `Annex B`); `""` if none. For an internal reference it is the part pointed at (`表 1`, `5.2`).

### 3.5 Target content and the named quantity

`target_content` says what this document takes from the target:

| Value | Use when |
|---|---|
| `criterion` | the limit, grade or required value of **one named measurable quantity**, which this document does not state itself ("粪大肠菌群数应符合 GB 7959 的要求") |
| `criteria_set` | the limits of a **set** of quantities, given by a locator or placeholder ("其他指标应符合 GB 7959 表 1 的 II 类") |
| `method` | how something is measured, tested, sampled, calculated or determined, even when a quantity is named ("额定功率按 GB/T 755 测定") |
| `term` | the meaning of terms ("GB/T 19000 界定的术语和定义适用于本文件") |
| `requirements` | a body of requirements as a whole, with no named quantity ("收运应符合 CJJ 184") |
| `data` | values or findings this document **states itself** and credits to the target |
| `general` | nothing specific: further reading, "see also", supersession, adoption, and **every listed entry** |

`criterion` needs all three: (1) a named measurable quantity of an object (a value on a unit or
scale; a process, product or management system as a whole is not one); (2) the text takes the
criterion, not the method; (3) this document does not state the value. Decide from what the
sentence does, not from its verbs.

**Internal references are never `criterion` or `criteria_set` (benchmark rule)**: the values they
point at are this document's own and are extracted as its metrics. "应符合表 1 的规定" is
`requirements` with `target_locator = 表 1`.

For `criterion` only, give the named quantity: `metric_name`, `metric_subject` (the object it
belongs to) and `condition` (stated applicability, `""` if none), in the document's language; for
non-English documents also `metric_name_en` and `metric_subject_en`. All five are empty/null for
every other content. `criteria_set` names no single quantity: leave them empty.

### 3.6 Form, normativity, list links and mismatches

- `ref_form`: `listed` (A2) or `in_text` (everything else). `list_kind`: `normative` or
  `informative` for listed entries, `""` for in-text. `list_label`: the entry's label in its list
  (`[12]`, `12.`, `Smith et al. 2024`), `""` if the list has no labels or for in-text references.
- `in_requirement`: true when the reference sits inside a requirement of this document (shall,
  must, is required, 应, 必须, 不得, 不应). Permissive modality (宜, 可, should, may) is false.
  Listed entries are false.
- `list_entry_ref_id`: for an in-text, non-internal reference, the `ref_id` of the listed entry
  of the same resource; `""` if the resource is in no list. Same resource means the same identity
  key (§3.7) — edition differences do not matter. When both lists hold it, link the normative entry.
  A numbered or author-year citation links the entry with that label.
- `normativity`, **from the lists only, never from wording**: a listed entry takes its
  `list_kind`; an in-text reference takes the `list_kind` of its linked entry, or `unlisted` when it
  has none. Internal references are `unlisted`.
- `list_mismatch`, only when the document has a normative references list, otherwise `""`:
  - `not_listed` on an in-text, non-internal reference with `in_requirement = true`, normativity
    other than `normative`, and a public identifier (§3.7). Manufacturer's instructions and other
    unidentified material are never `not_listed`;
  - `never_cited` on a normative listed entry that no in-text reference links to.

`validate` recomputes `normativity`, the list links and both mismatches from the rows and refuses
any disagreement: fix the rows (identifiers, links, `in_requirement`), not the derived label.

### 3.7 Identifiers

`identifiers` is the list of identifiers of the cited resource, normalized the way
`reference_identifiers.go` does it: `[{"kind": "standard", "value": "GB/T 755", "edition": "2019"}]`.

- Kinds: `standard`, `rfc`, `legal`, `doi`, `arxiv`, `pmid`, `isbn`, `issn`, `patent`, `url` (all
  public), plus `file_path` and `repository` (private).
- A standard keeps publisher prefix, number and part in `value`, with one space before the number
  (`GB/T 755`, `ISO 14253-1`, `NY/T 1168`), and the edition year alone in `edition`
  (`GB/T755-2019` → `GB/T 755` + `2019`; `ISO 14253-1` has no edition). Omit `edition` when the
  citation is undated.
- DOIs are lower-case; URLs as written; RFC as `RFC 9110`; EU acts as `Regulation (EU) 2016/679`.
- A numbered or author-year citation carries the identifiers of its list entry.
- An internal or indirect reference, or a title-only citation, has `[]`.

The **identity key** of a reference is its first identifier in the order standard, rfc, legal,
doi, arxiv, pmid, isbn, issn, patent, url, as `kind:value`; otherwise `title:` plus the
normalized `target_title`. It decides "same resource" in §3.6.

### 3.8 Confidence

`confidence` is a finite number from 0 to 1 for the reviewer's certainty; it never replaces review.

## 4. Audit completeness, correctness and consistency separately

1. **Completeness:** re-read each section without relying on the drafted rows. Then search, as a
   second pass, for: standard prefixes (GB, GB/T, ISO, IEC, EN, ASTM, CJJ, HJ, NY, T/…), 《…》
   titles, `[n]`, `(Author, year)`, URLs and DOIs, "按/依据/根据/参照/符合/见/引用/采用/代替",
   "according to / in accordance with / as specified in / see", and internal pointers ("表 N",
   "图 N", "附录 X", "第 N 章", "N.N 条", "式(N)"). Reconcile every hit with a row or an explained
   exclusion. Check that every reference list line is an A2 row, that a sentence citing two targets
   or taking two contents gave two rows, and that tables, notes and footnotes were read.
2. **Correctness:** verify every field against the cited lines: `cited_raw` as written, identifiers
   normalized (§3.7), type and subtype, mechanism, relationships, scope and locator,
   `target_content` by the three `criterion` tests (method vs criterion vs requirements vs data),
   the named quantity on `criterion` rows only, `in_requirement` by modality, internal pointers
   labelled internal.
3. **Consistency:** group rows by identity key and by sentence. The same kind of clause gets the
   same labels ("收运应符合 CJJ 184" and "处理应符合 CJJ 52" both `requirements`/`complies_with`).
   Every in-text citation of a listed resource links its entry; every A2 row is in exactly one list;
   check that no excluded candidate (X2, X4, X6) is accepted elsewhere for the same wording, and the
   reverse. Re-decide both sides of a conflict; do not edit rationales to fit.

Describe concrete checks and findings in `completeness_review` and `correctness_review`; put the
consistency findings in `correctness_review` under a "Consistency:" heading. Resolve all uncertain
candidates; never relabel uncertainty as exclusion to pass validation. If parser damage, missing
context or a rules gap remains, keep the draft and name it in `limitations`; do not save.
